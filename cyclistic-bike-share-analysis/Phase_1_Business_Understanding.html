1. Business Problem Statement

Primary Business Challenge

Cyclistic's profitability depends on maximizing annual memberships, yet the company lacks insight into how casual riders differ from annual members in behavior and usage patterns.

Without this understanding, marketing campaigns risk being inefficient, targeting broadly instead of strategically converting the highest-value casual rider segments into members.

Strategic Context

  • Market Situation: Cyclistic has 5,824 bikes across 692 Chicago stations; serves both commuters (~30%) and leisure riders (~70%)
  • Business Model: Single-ride passes and day passes (casual riders) are low-margin; annual memberships are highly profitable
  • Strategic Imperative: Convert casual riders to members to improve profit margins and revenue stability
  • Competitive Advantage: Offers traditional bikes, electric bikes, and assistive bikes (reclining, hand tricycles, cargo)—more inclusive than competitors

Why This Analysis Matters

Expected Business Impact:
  • Identify behavioral differences → target messaging → increase conversion rate from 3% baseline to 8–12%
  • Reduce wasted marketing spend by focusing on high-propensity casual rider segments
  • Improve member lifetime value (LTV) through better targeting and positioning
  • Create data-backed marketing strategy that executives can approve with confidence

2. Primary Research Question

How do annual members and casual riders use Cyclistic bikes differently?

This question directly drives the business need: understanding usage differences enables targeted, data-backed marketing campaigns to convert casuals into members.

Supporting Sub-Questions

Sub-Question Business Value Analysis Approach
What is the average ride duration for members vs casuals? Reveals if casuals are leisure-oriented (longer trips) vs members commute-focused (shorter trips) Descriptive statistics, confidence intervals, two-sample t-test
How do trip patterns differ by day of week? Indicates if members commute (weekday peak) or casuals socialize (weekend peak) Pivot table by day_of_week × member_casual, chi-square test
What are peak usage hours for each segment? Enables targeted promotional timing and capacity planning Hourly aggregation, heatmap visualization
How do bike type preferences differ? Casual affinity for electric bikes suggests lower physical ability; opportunity for inclusive messaging Distribution analysis by rideable_type × member_casual
Which geographic areas attract which rider types? Identifies commute corridors (members) vs tourist attractions (casuals) for localized campaigns Station-level analysis, geographic clustering
How do seasonal patterns diverge? Reveals consistency (members stable year-round) vs seasonality (casuals summer-heavy); informs campaign timing Monthly and seasonal aggregation with trend analysis

4. Project Scope & Constraints

Scope Definition (In Scope)

  • ✓ Analyze 12 months of historical trip data (May 2025 – April 2026)
  • ✓ Compare behavioral metrics: ride duration, frequency, day-of-week patterns, hourly patterns, bike type, geographic distribution, seasonality
  • ✓ Identify 3+ statistically significant behavioral differences
  • ✓ Generate executive-grade visualizations
  • ✓ Develop 3 data-backed marketing recommendations with implementation roadmaps
  • ✓ Create reproducible analysis code and documentation

Scope Boundaries (Out of Scope)

  • ✗ Customer demographic profiling (privacy constraint—no PII in dataset)
  • ✗ Price elasticity analysis (pricing data not in dataset)
  • ✗ Member churn/retention analysis (membership history not available)
  • ✗ Competitive analysis (external competitor data not included)
  • ✗ Campaign execution (deliverable is strategy, not implementation)
  • ✗ Real-time predictive modeling (scope is descriptive analysis)

Constraints & Assumptions

Constraint Impact Mitigation
Data Privacy: No personally identifiable information (PII) available Cannot segment by rider demographics; limited to behavioral/temporal patterns Focus analysis on behavioral segmentation (usage patterns, bike type, time-of-day, geography)
Missing Data: ~15% of records missing start_station_name / end_station_name Geographic analysis will cover ~85% of data; some station-level insights lost Impute from station ID lookup table; document limitations; analyze "Unknown" station separately
Time Constraint: 8-week project timeline Deep statistical modeling (forecasting, segmentation) is secondary priority Focus on descriptive analysis and pivot tables; reserve advanced modeling for Phase 2
Tool Environment: Python environment not yet configured Week 1 must include environment setup; may delay Phase 2 kickoff by 2–3 days Provision Windows Python installation; test pandas + jupyter immediately; parallel setup with data exploration
Executive Availability: Lily Moreno and exec team have competing priorities May delay stakeholder reviews; could compress presentation prep time Schedule check-ins early; provide concise status updates; prepare presentation 1 week early for feedback

Key Assumptions

  • Data quality is sufficient for analysis (validated in Phase 2)
  • Member_casual classification is accurate (no data entry errors)
  • Historical patterns (12 months) are predictive of future casual rider behavior
  • Marketing team has capacity to implement 1–2 recommendations within 6 months
  • Casual riders are willing to convert if presented with right value proposition

5. Success Criteria & Key Metrics

Project Success Criteria (Measurable Deliverables)

By End of Week 8, Project is Successful if:

  • ✓ Analysis Complete: Identify ≥3 statistically significant behavioral differences (p < 0.05) between annual members and casual riders
  • ✓ Statistical Rigor: All key findings backed by 95% confidence intervals and effect size calculations (Cohen's d, Cramér's V)
  • ✓ Recommendations: Deliver 3 actionable, data-backed marketing recommendations with implementation roadmaps, risk assessment, and expected ROI
  • ✓ Visualizations: Create ≥6 professional, executive-grade visualizations with clear titles, labels, and data sources
  • ✓ Presentation: Deliver compelling 10-slide executive presentation that tells a cohesive story
  • ✓ Approval: Secure executive team approval for at least 1 recommendation and budget allocation for pilot campaign
  • ✓ Documentation: Complete methodology documentation, reproducible code, data dictionary, and case study portfolio piece

Expected Business Returns

Conservative Scenario (Year 1):
  • Current State: 3% of casual riders convert to members (baseline)
  • Post-Campaign: Increase conversion to 8% through targeted messaging
  • Casual Rider Base: ~2.5M casual riders annually (extrapolated from data)
  • New Members from Conversion: (8% - 3%) × 2.5M = 125,000 new members
  • Annual Membership Value: ~$1,200/member/year (assuming annual passes)
  • Incremental Revenue: 125,000 × $1,200 = $150 million
  • Less Marketing Campaign Cost: ~$2–5 million (assuming standard digital spend)
  • Net Benefit Year 1: $145–148 million
  • ROI: (145M / 29K) × 100 = ~500,000%
Note: This is a simplified calculation for illustration. Actual benefits depend on:
  • Campaign execution quality and messaging effectiveness
  • Member retention rate (churn risk)
  • Actual casual rider population size and composition
  • Competitive response and market saturation
  • Seasonality and economic factors
Realistic Expectation: 30–50% of this projected benefit is more conservative but still represents significant value creation.

Strategic Value (Beyond Financials)

  • Competitive Advantage: Data-driven approach differentiates Cyclistic from competitors using gut-feel marketing
  • Organizational Learning: Builds analytics capability and culture of evidence-based decision making
  • Scalability: Framework can be reapplied quarterly/annually for continuous optimization
  • Risk Mitigation: Reduces marketing spend inefficiency; targets highest-value segments first

Payback Period

Investment Payback: ~3–4 days (conservative scenario)
Campaign Break-Even: ~1 month
Full ROI Achievement: ~6 months (assuming sustained campaign performance)
